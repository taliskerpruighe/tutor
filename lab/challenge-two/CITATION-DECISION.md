# Citation Decision — Contract 4 (Merrow Cardiology Associates / Fenwick)

## Decision

**Omitted.** Contract 4 contains no statutory citation, section number, chapter
number, public act number, or reference to any physician-specific noncompete
statute. Connecticut's physician-noncompete rule (which imposes a numeric
duration/mileage cap and other conditions on restrictive covenants with
physicians) is not cited, named, or paraphrased with any numeric authority
in the document.

## Reason

I have no web access in this session and there is no statute source file in
this repository. A section number or a numeric cap I could not verify against
primary source text would be a fabricated citation, which is worse than no
citation at all. Rather than guess at a chapter or section number, I omitted
the statutory reference entirely and expressed the physician-specific
narrowing purely as drafting choices — term, geography, scope, and an express
carve-out — stated in plain contract English with no authority cited.

## How the physician narrowing is expressed instead (plain contract terms)

Contract 4 is deliberately the narrowest of the nine agreements in the corpus,
carrying no citation to justify that narrowness — the narrowness itself is
the tell. Specifically:

**Short term (twelve months) and small radius (fifteen miles), stated as an
ordinary business restriction with no authority cited:**

> "During Employee's employment with the Company and for a period of twelve
> (12) months following the termination of Employee's employment with the
> Company for any reason (the "Restricted Period"), Employee shall not,
> directly or indirectly, practice interventional cardiology, or provide
> physician services substantially similar to those Employee provided to the
> Company, within a fifteen (15) mile radius of the Company's primary
> practice site identified above (the "Practice Site")..."

**A reasonableness acknowledgment tying the restriction to a narrow,
legitimate interest, again with no authority cited:**

> "Employee acknowledges that this restriction is limited to the specific
> specialty in which Employee practiced for the Company, is confined to a
> small geographic area immediately surrounding the Practice Site, and is
> intended only to protect the Company's legitimate interest in the
> continuity of the patient and referral relationships it has developed in
> that limited area, without impeding Employee's ability to practice
> medicine, including interventional cardiology, outside that area."

**A termination-without-cause carve-out — the only one of the nine contracts
that has one — voiding the patient-facing restriction if the practice lets
her go without cause (library clause 12j, pasted verbatim):**

> "Termination Without Cause. Notwithstanding any other provision of this
> Agreement, if the Company terminates Employee's employment without Cause
> (as defined in the Company's standard physician employment policies), the
> post-employment restriction on treating patients described in the
> Non-Solicitation of Patients Section above shall not apply, and Employee
> shall remain free to treat any patient of the practice following such a
> termination."

**A citation-free continuity-of-care provision, addressing the same concern
a physician-specific statute would address (patient abandonment), entirely
in contract terms:**

> "(A) Employee shall not abandon any patient who is in an active course of
> treatment with Employee as of the date Employee's employment with the
> Company ends. Employee shall cooperate with the Company in a reasonable
> transition of the care of such patients to another physician of the
> Company or, where the patient elects, to a physician of the patient's own
> choosing.
>
> (B) Nothing in this Agreement prohibits Employee from notifying patients
> of Employee's departure from the Company in accordance with applicable
> professional and licensing standards, or from responding to a patient's
> request for copies of the patient's own medical records or for
> information about the patient's continuing care, regardless of whether
> the patient is later treated by Employee."

Together, the short term, small radius, without-cause carve-out, and
continuity-of-care provision let a reader see that this agreement was
drafted with physician-specific restraint in mind, without the document
ever needing to name the rule that motivated it.
