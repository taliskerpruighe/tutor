# Shared Provisions Library — Challenge Two Noncompete Corpus

This file is the single source of clause text for the nine fictional
noncompete agreements in this teaching corpus. Three authoring workers will
each draft three contracts, in parallel and blind to one another. To keep the
corpus internally consistent, **paste the assigned clauses from this file
verbatim** into your source documents — do not rewrite them, even lightly,
unless a clause below is explicitly offered as a variant and you are told to
choose one variant per contract.

Every clause below is fictional, drafted for a teaching exercise. It is not
legal advice and must not be used as an actual contract template.

See `lab/challenge-two/sources/manifest.tsv` for which contract uses which
variant/style of each clause below. Do not invent your own phrasing for any
provision type that appears in this library — use what's here, choosing the
assigned variant/style per the manifest.

**No case citations, no reporter citations, and no statute section numbers
appear anywhere below.** Doctrine (reformation power, business-interest
framing, lookback periods, and so on) is expressed only as drafting choices,
never as a citation to authority. Keep it that way if you add anything.

**Placeholder substitution.** Several clauses below contain a `[TERM]`
placeholder. Replace it with your contract's post-employment restricted
period, taken from the `term` column of `manifest.tsv` (e.g. "12 months"
for contract 1). This applies to the customer non-solicitation clauses
(Section 2) and the employee no-hire clauses (Section 5). One exception:
in Style B (Section 2b), the twenty-four-month figure inside the
definition of "Lookback Period" is a fixed drafting feature of that style
and is independent of the contract's restricted-period term — do not
change it, even for a contract (such as 3, 6 months, or 9, 9 months) whose
own restricted period is shorter than the twenty-four-month lookback. A
lookback window longer than the restriction that follows it is realistic
drafting, not an error to fix.

## 1. Confidentiality / Trade Secrets

### 1a. Canonical

```
Confidentiality and Trade Secrets. Employee acknowledges that, in the course
of employment with the Company, Employee will have access to and may create
Confidential Information, and Employee agrees to hold all Confidential
Information in strict confidence and not to use or disclose any Confidential
Information except as required in the good-faith performance of Employee's
duties for the Company. "Confidential Information" means all non-public
information relating to the Company's business, including without
limitation customer lists and customer data, pricing and cost information,
business and marketing plans, financial information, product designs and
specifications, research and development materials, supplier and vendor
information, personnel information, software, know-how, and any other
information that derives independent economic value from not being
generally known to, and not being readily ascertainable by proper means by,
other persons who can obtain economic value from its disclosure or use,
together with all trade secrets as that term is understood under applicable
law. Employee's obligations under this Section survive termination of
employment for any reason and continue for so long as the information
in question retains the character of Confidential Information or a trade
secret, without limitation as to time for trade secrets. Upon termination
of employment, or at any earlier time upon the Company's request, Employee
shall return or destroy all documents, files, and other materials, in
whatever form, containing or reflecting any Confidential Information.
```

### 1b. Variant 1 — obligation-first, enumerated carve-outs

```
Nondisclosure of Company Information. During employment and at all times
thereafter, Employee shall not use, disclose, publish, or make accessible
to any third party any Proprietary Information, except to the extent
necessary to perform Employee's assigned duties for the Company or as
specifically authorized in writing by an officer of the Company. Proprietary
Information includes, without limitation: (i) technical information such
as product formulations, engineering data, designs, prototypes, and
research results; (ii) business information such as customer and prospect
identities and requirements, pricing, margins, supplier terms, and
strategic or financial plans; and (iii) any other nonpublic information
that the Company treats as confidential. Employee's obligations under this
Section do not apply to information that (a) is or becomes generally known
to the public through no fault of Employee, (b) was lawfully in Employee's
possession before disclosure by the Company, free of any obligation of
confidence, or (c) is required to be disclosed by valid legal process,
provided Employee gives the Company prompt notice and a reasonable
opportunity to seek a protective order before disclosure occurs. Nothing
in this Section limits any protection afforded to trade secrets under
applicable law, and as to trade secrets this Section's restrictions
continue for as long as the information remains a trade secret.
```

### 1c. Variant 2 — plain narrative

```
Company Secrets. While you work for the Company, and after your employment
ends for any reason, you agree to keep private everything you learn about
the Company's business that isn't already public — things like who its
customers are and what they buy, how the Company prices its work, its
plans for new products or new markets, and how its internal systems and
processes work. You will not tell anyone outside the Company about any of
this, and you will not use it to benefit yourself or anyone other than the
Company, unless your job requires it or the Company tells you in writing
that it's all right to share it. When your employment ends, you agree to
give back — or, if the Company asks, destroy — anything in your possession
that contains this kind of information, including notes, files, drafts,
and copies, in any format. Where any of this information also qualifies
as a trade secret under applicable law, this promise continues for as
long as it remains a trade secret, with no end date.
```

## 2. Customer Non-Solicitation

### 2a. Style A — Schedule / Enumerated

```
Non-Solicitation of Scheduled Customers. During employment and for a period
of [TERM] following the termination of Employee's employment for any
reason, Employee shall not, directly or indirectly, solicit, call upon,
or accept business from any customer identified on Schedule A attached to
and made a part of this Agreement, for the purpose of providing any product
or service competitive with those offered by the Company. Schedule A may
be updated by the Company from time to time during Employee's employment
by written notice to Employee to reflect changes in Employee's customer
responsibilities, and the version of Schedule A in effect on the date
Employee's employment terminates shall govern this Section. Employee
acknowledges that the customers listed on Schedule A represent
relationships the Company has invested substantial time and expense to
develop and that Employee's access to those relationships was a direct
result of Employee's employment with the Company.
```

### 2b. Style B — Lookback / Material Contact

```
Non-Solicitation of Customers. For purposes of this Section, "Restricted
Customer" means any person or entity that (i) was a customer of the
Company at any time during the twenty-four (24) months immediately
preceding the termination of Employee's employment (the "Lookback
Period"), and (ii) with whom Employee had Material Contact during the
Lookback Period. "Material Contact" means contact between Employee and a
customer (a) with whom Employee dealt on the Company's behalf, (b) whose
dealings with the Company were coordinated or supervised by Employee, (c)
about whom Employee obtained Confidential Information in the ordinary
course of business as a result of Employee's association with the
Company, or (d) who received products or services from the Company, the
sale or provision of which resulted in compensation, commissions, or
earnings for Employee within the Lookback Period. During employment and
for a period of [TERM] thereafter, Employee shall not, on behalf of
Employee or any other person or entity, solicit, divert, or attempt to
solicit or divert the business of any Restricted Customer for the
purpose of providing products or services competitive with those offered
by the Company during the Lookback Period.
```

### 2c. Style C — Plain English

```
Not Taking Clients With You. When you leave the Company, whether you
resign or are let go, you agree that for [TERM] after your last day you
will not try to take the Company's clients with you. That means you will
not reach out to any client you worked with or knew about because of your
job here to get them to move their business away from the Company, and
you will not accept business from one of those clients if they reach out
to you first about the kind of work the Company does. This isn't meant to
stop you from working somewhere else or in the same industry — it's just
meant to stop you from using the relationships you built here to pull the
Company's clients away when you go.
```

*(Contract 4 uses Style C reframed for a physician's patients — replace
"clients" with "patients" and "business" with "care," and keep the
remainder of the structure and tone unchanged.)*

*(Contracts 1 and 9 use Style A. Each must append its own Schedule A — a
short attached list of three to six invented customer names, consistent
with that contract's industry — as an exhibit at the end of the source
document. Do not merely reference an unattached schedule; include it, so
that the rendered contract is a complete, self-contained document.)*

## 4. Severability

### 4a. Connecticut express-reformation

```
Severability; Reformation. If any restriction in this Agreement is found
by a court of competent jurisdiction to be unenforceable because it is
unreasonable as to scope, duration, or geographic area, or for any other
reason, the parties intend for the court to modify and reform that
restriction, rather than to void it entirely, so as to render it
enforceable to the maximum extent the court deems reasonable, and the
restriction shall then be enforced as so modified. The parties expressly
authorize a reviewing court to narrow the scope, shorten the duration, or
reduce the geographic area of any restriction in this Agreement to the
extent necessary to make it enforceable, and agree that such judicial
modification shall not affect the enforceability of the remainder of this
Agreement. If any other provision of this Agreement is held invalid or
unenforceable, the remaining provisions shall continue in full force and
effect.
```

### 4b. New Jersey reformation

```
Severability; Reformation; Legitimate Business Interest. The parties agree
that the restrictions in this Agreement are reasonable and necessary to
protect the Company's legitimate business interests, including its
confidential information, customer relationships, and goodwill, and are
reasonably limited in time, geography, and scope to protect those
interests without imposing an undue hardship on Employee or injuring the
public. If, notwithstanding the foregoing, any restriction is found by a
court to exceed what is reasonably necessary to protect the Company's
legitimate business interests, the parties agree that the court shall
reform the restriction to the maximum time, geographic, and scope
limitations that the court determines are reasonable and necessary to
protect those interests, and shall enforce the restriction as reformed.
The invalidity or unenforceability of any provision of this Agreement
shall not affect the validity or enforceability of any other provision.
```

### 4c. Bare severability, no reformation power

```
Severability. If any provision of this Agreement is held by a court of
competent jurisdiction to be invalid, illegal, or unenforceable, that
provision shall be struck from this Agreement, and the remaining
provisions shall continue in full force and effect according to their
terms.
```

## 5. Employee No-Hire / Non-Solicitation of Employees

Not every contract includes this provision. Check the `employee_nonsolicit`
column of `manifest.tsv` for your assigned contracts: it reads `canonical
(5a)`, `variant (5b)`, or `omitted`. If it says `omitted`, do not add an
employee no-hire clause to that contract at all.

### 5a. Canonical

```
Non-Solicitation of Employees. During employment and for a period of
[TERM] following the termination of Employee's employment for any reason,
Employee shall not, directly or indirectly, solicit, recruit, or induce,
or attempt to solicit, recruit, or induce, any employee of the Company to
terminate his or her employment with the Company, or to accept employment
with any other person or entity, nor shall Employee hire or engage, or
assist any other person or entity in hiring or engaging, any person who
was an employee of the Company at any time during the six (6) months
preceding the termination of Employee's employment. This Section applies
only to employees with whom Employee had contact, about whom Employee
obtained Confidential Information, or whom Employee supervised, in each
case during the last twelve (12) months of Employee's employment with the
Company.
```

### 5b. Variant

```
No Poaching Company Staff. For [TERM] after your employment ends, you
agree not to try to convince any of the Company's employees to quit and
come work somewhere else with you, and you agree not to help any new
employer of yours hire them away, whether that means recruiting them
directly, passing along their contact information, or telling a recruiter
who to call. This applies to people you worked with, managed, or knew
about through your job at the Company in your last year there.
```

## 6. Injunctive Relief

```
Injunctive Relief. Employee acknowledges that a breach or threatened
breach of the restrictive covenants in this Agreement would cause the
Company irreparable harm for which money damages would not be an adequate
remedy. Accordingly, in the event of any such breach or threatened breach,
the Company shall be entitled, in addition to any other rights and
remedies available at law or in equity, to seek temporary, preliminary,
and permanent injunctive relief restraining Employee from committing or
continuing any such breach, without the necessity of posting a bond or
other security and without the necessity of proving actual damages.
Nothing in this Section shall be construed to limit the Company's right
to pursue any other remedy available to it, including the recovery of
damages.
```

## 7. Tolling During Breach

```
Tolling. If Employee breaches any restrictive covenant contained in this
Agreement, the period of restriction applicable to that covenant shall be
extended, or tolled, by the length of time during which Employee was in
breach, so that the Company receives the full benefit of the bargained-for
restriction. This tolling shall apply automatically and without the need
for further action by the Company, and shall run from the date a court of
competent jurisdiction determines that a breach occurred, or from the date
the breach ceases, whichever the Company elects.
```

## 9. At-Will Disclaimer

```
At-Will Employment. Employee's employment with the Company is at-will,
meaning that either Employee or the Company may terminate the employment
relationship at any time, with or without cause and with or without
notice. Nothing in this Agreement shall be construed to alter Employee's
at-will employment status, to guarantee employment for any specific
duration, or to create any express or implied contract of continued
employment.
```

## 10. Return of Company Property

```
Return of Company Property. Upon termination of employment for any
reason, or at any earlier time upon the Company's request, Employee shall
promptly return to the Company all property belonging to the Company,
including without limitation all documents, files, records, computers,
mobile devices, access cards, keys, and any other equipment or materials,
together with all copies thereof, in whatever medium, and shall not
retain any copies, extracts, or summaries of any of the foregoing.
```

```
Assignment of Inventions. Employee agrees that all inventions,
```

## 12. Ancillary Provisions Library (choose per manifest assignment)

These are shorter, standard clauses. Each contract's manifest row lists
which of these appear in it. Use the text below verbatim for whichever are
assigned.

### 12a. Non-Disparagement

```
Non-Disparagement. Employee agrees not to make any statement, oral or
written, that disparages or is reasonably likely to damage the business
reputation or goodwill of the Company, its products, or its officers,
directors, or employees, except as required by law or in connection with
a good-faith report to, or testimony before, a governmental agency or in
a legal proceeding.
```

### 12b. Attorneys' Fees

```
Attorneys' Fees. In any action to enforce the restrictive covenants in
this Agreement, the prevailing party shall be entitled to recover its
reasonable attorneys' fees and costs from the non-prevailing party, in
addition to any other relief to which it may be entitled.
```

### 12c. Assignment and Successors

```
Assignment; Successors. The Company may assign this Agreement, without
Employee's consent, to any successor to all or substantially all of the
business or assets of the Company, whether by merger, sale of assets, or
otherwise, and this Agreement shall inure to the benefit of and be
binding upon any such successor or assign. Employee may not assign this
Agreement or any rights or obligations under it.
```

### 12d. Survival

```
Survival. The provisions of this Agreement that by their terms are
intended to apply after termination of employment, including without
limitation the confidentiality, non-solicitation, and intellectual
property provisions, shall survive the termination of Employee's
employment and the expiration of this Agreement.
```

### 12e. Notice

```
Notice. Any notice required or permitted under this Agreement shall be in
writing and shall be deemed given when delivered personally, sent by
overnight courier, or sent by certified mail, return receipt requested,
to the address of the recipient last known to the sender.
```

### 12f. Jury Waiver

```
Waiver of Jury Trial. Each party irrevocably waives, to the fullest
extent permitted by applicable law, any right to a trial by jury in any
action, proceeding, or counterclaim arising out of or relating to this
Agreement.
```

### 12g. Forum Selection

```
Forum Selection. Each party consents to the exclusive jurisdiction of the
state and federal courts located in the state whose law governs this
Agreement under the Governing Law Section above, for any action arising
out of or relating to this Agreement, and waives any objection to venue
or personal jurisdiction in those courts.
```

### 12h. Mandatory Arbitration (contract 3 only)

```
Arbitration. Except for claims seeking injunctive relief under the
Injunctive Relief Section above, any dispute arising out of or relating
to this Agreement shall be resolved by binding arbitration administered
by a neutral arbitrator in accordance with the commercial arbitration
rules then in effect for the state whose law governs this Agreement, with
the arbitration to be held in the county where the Company's office
identified in this Agreement is located. The arbitrator's decision shall
be final and binding, and judgment on the award may be entered in any
court having jurisdiction.
```

### 12i. Garden Leave / Paid Notice Period (contract 7 only)

```
Garden Leave. The Company may, in its discretion, place Employee on
garden leave for up to ninety (90) days following notice of termination
of employment by either party, during which time Employee shall continue
to receive base salary and benefits but shall not be required or
permitted to perform any duties for the Company, shall not accept other
employment, and shall remain bound by all restrictive covenants in this
Agreement. Any period of garden leave shall run concurrently with, and
shall not extend, the post-employment restricted period described
elsewhere in this Agreement.
```

### 12j. Termination-Without-Cause Carve-Out (contract 4 only)

```
Termination Without Cause. Notwithstanding any other provision of this
Agreement, if the Company terminates Employee's employment without Cause
(as defined in the Company's standard physician employment policies),
none of the post-employment restrictions in this Agreement shall apply,
including both the restriction on practicing within the restricted
geographic area described elsewhere in this Agreement and the restriction
on treating patients described in the Non-Solicitation of Patients
Section above, and Employee shall remain free to practice within the
restricted geographic area and to treat any patient of the practice
following such a termination.
```

*(This carve-out releases the full restrictive-covenant package for
contract 4 — both the geographic noncompete and the patient
non-solicitation — not just one of the two. This is what makes contract 4
the narrowest of the nine: the restriction only ever binds an employee who
is terminated for cause or who resigns voluntarily.)*

## DO NOT WRITE

The following two provision types must appear in **no contract**, in any
phrasing, paraphrase, or partial form. They are deliberate gaps the
exercise depends on:

1. **Supplier / vendor non-solicitation.** No contract may restrict
   Employee from soliciting, contacting, poaching, or doing business with
   any of the Company's suppliers, vendors, or distributors, in any form.
   Do not include this even as a passing reference inside another clause
   (for example, do not fold suppliers into the customer non-solicitation
   clause).

2. **Training-cost repayment / clawback.** No contract may require
   Employee to repay the Company for sponsored training, tuition,
   certification costs, licensing costs, or any similar expense if
   Employee departs early. Do not include this even as a passing mention
   inside another clause (for example, do not fold it into the return of
   Company property or integration clauses).

If you are drafting a contract and find yourself reaching for either of
these two ideas, stop — they are intentionally absent from this corpus.
