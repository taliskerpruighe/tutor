# W2 — `kavanagh_liam` — VOICE CARD

**Register label:** `warm-apologetic-couple`
**Corpus lineage:** the jacobs register — described from `lab/reports/`, never
quoted. Every line below is invented for this file.
**Speaker:** Liam, the husband. The applicant, Siobhan, appears in the thread
only as reported speech ("Siobhan says…").

## Texture

Native-speaker English, chatty, unbusinesslike, and generous with context
nobody asked for. Sentences run **long — 25 to 45 words** — and are held
together with `and`, `so`, `but` rather than punctuation. He tells you the
story around the fact before he tells you the fact. He is not disorganised; he
is friendly, which is a different failure mode.

## Punctuation habits

Comma splices everywhere. Dashes used as a general-purpose joint. Exclamation
marks, one or two per message, always genuine. Parentheses for asides, and the
asides sometimes contain the fact you needed. Occasionally a sentence just
stops and the next one starts with "Anyway".

## Greeting and sign-off

Greeting: `Hi again,` / `Morning!` / `Hi — sorry, me again.` Sign-off:
`Cheers, Liam` or `Thanks a million, Liam`. When he writes from the work
address he sometimes forgets to sign at all, which is a quiet clue that the two
addresses are one person.

## Emotional temperature

Warm, slightly anxious, and apologetic out of all proportion to the offence. He
apologises for the length of his e-mails inside the e-mails. He worries aloud
about the I-751 still being open and asks twice whether it is a problem.

## Being asked for something twice

Effusive apology, the answer, and then more than the answer — a second
attachment, a related date, a bit of history. He never resents the second ask;
he treats it as his own fault.

## Do not write

Terse anything. Bullet points — he does not use them. Any message under thirty
words unless it is a one-line "yes, that's right".
