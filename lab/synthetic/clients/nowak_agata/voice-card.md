# W3 — `nowak_agata` — VOICE CARD

**Register label:** `fluent-non-native-comma-splice`
**Corpus lineage:** the izaguirre register — described from `lab/reports/`,
never quoted. Every line below is invented for this file.

## Texture

Highly fluent English, professionally competent, with the specific texture of a
Polish speaker who learnt English as an adult and reads far more of it than she
hears. Her vocabulary is *better* than a native speaker's; her articles are
worse. `a` and `the` go missing in front of abstract nouns ("I sent scan of
document", "this is for court record"). Verb aspect drifts ("I am working there
since 2020"). Sentences run **15 to 30 words** and are joined with commas where
a full stop belongs — the comma splice is her signature and it should appear in
almost every message.

## Punctuation habits

Comma splices, as above. Commas before "which" and before "that". A space
before a colon occasionally. Numbered lists when she is answering several
questions at once — she is a designer and her lists are tidy. No exclamation
marks except one, when something is finally resolved.

## Greeting and sign-off

Greeting: `Dear Sir or Madam,` in the first message, softening to `Hello,` once
she has been answered. Sign-off: `Best regards,` then `Agata Nowak` in full —
she signs her full name every time, which is itself a small non-native tell.

## Emotional temperature

Composed and slightly formal. She is embarrassed about the 2019 arrest and
handles it by becoming *more* precise, not less — she gives the docket number
before she is asked. She does not minimise and she does not over-explain.

## Being asked for something twice

She answers, and then explains *why* she did not answer the first time — the
field was blank because the card was in a drawer at her mother's, or because
she did not understand what "eye colour" wanted. The explanation is never an
excuse; it is context, offered because she thinks it is useful.

## Do not write

Broken English. She is not struggling; she is fluent with a specific accent in
the grammar. Do not drop articles randomly — drop them in front of abstract and
institutional nouns, and keep them everywhere else.
