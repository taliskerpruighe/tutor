# jacobs_brent — intake chronology (runner report)

## Arc
2015 AOS -> 2016 I-130 denial + I-290B motion -> 2017 approval -> 2018 I-751
-> 2025 N-400 -> Sept–Nov 2025 chasing USCIS for a case number.

## How input actually arrives
- **2015 (AOS): ad hoc.** No formal questionnaire. Client emails phone and
  address as prose in an email body. Then a bulk forward titled "AOS Stuff"
  with a scattergun of PDFs: G-325A, travel history, insurance policy, visa
  confirmation page, airline e-tickets (Delta, Webjet), ESTA approval, hotel
  bookings, CGI Federal payment confirmation, two certificates of insurance.
  Tax returns arrive separately, later. Medical exam handled by physical
  pickup from a Milwaukee clinic.
- **2015-08-24:** client photographs the USCIS biometrics letter with a phone
  and emails the JPG, asking what to bring "just in case".
- **2025 (N-400): formalised.** Firm sends engagement letter and invoice via
  DocuSign, plus a password-protected Airtable intake form:
  `https://airtable.com/appbxOQkyqZxbeZx0/pagzjhE14SKSpJKjw/form`
  Firm note, quotable: "The questionnaire is usually much longer; we went
  through it and cut the questions that we know we can already answer--and
  whose answers have not changed--from the old green card paperwork."
  Client returns it but flags education dates as estimates.

## Messiness worth imitating
- Client answers in prose and hedges ("dates are estimates, should I check
  with the school?").
- Client sends phone photos alongside proper scans.
- Draft form goes back and forth six times over 24 days.
- Client, not the firm, catches the errors: middle names in the First Name
  box, a stale email address, a mis-ticked checkbox on page 10, uncertainty
  over which parts need signing.
- Client asks a genuinely ambiguous eligibility question: does working in a
  prison count for Part 9 Q9?
- Delivery friction: form too large to email, moved to Google Drive; client
  asks for a print-and-scan instead to avoid "PDF glitches or omissions".
- Client uses several email addresses inconsistently (gmail, outlook, a
  wisconsin.gov work address).

## Client voice
Short, 2–5 sentences. Polite, apologetic, grateful. "Just checking in",
"Have a great day!". Occasional typos ("Sorry its taken me"). Escalates
politely but persistently when ignored.
