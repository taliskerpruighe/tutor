## THE MOST VALUABLE FINDING IN THE CORPUS: transcription loss
A completed client questionnaire survives beside the filed N-400, so the
transcription step can be audited directly. It is lossy:

| | questionnaire | N-400 as filed |
|---|---|---|
| surname | Malone | "Ma Lone" (space inserted) |
| street | 917 E 10th St | 917 E 16th St |
| apartment | #301 | #391 |
| addresses | 5, with dates | 4, dates mostly blank |
| employers | 5, with dates | 3, dates blank |
| children | 5 (incl. Dacearray Braun, b. 1988) | 4 |
| height/weight/hair/eyes | 5'6", 126 lbs, black & grey, brown | all blank |

Two employers inside the five-year window (Grandma's Restaurant; University of
Minnesota Duluth) simply vanish. One child vanishes. Digits transpose.

**For the synthetic build this is the single most important mechanic: the
output is not a faithful function of the input. A plugin that reproduces the
firm's work must reproduce a transcription, and the examples show what a real
transcription costs.** Whether the synthetic pairs should be clean or should
carry deliberate loss is a decision for the plan.

## What DOES carry across cleanly

dates and destinations. David's single trip aligns with his mother's third —
they travelled together, and the two forms agree. David's address history
mirrors his mother's for the shared-household periods.

## Rule set — confirmed, with a caveat
ID documents supply identity; questionnaire supplies history; firm derives
basis and continuous residence from the LPR date; firm supplies the preparer
block. Confirmed. The caveat is that "supplies" does not mean "supplies
completely" — Part 15 preparer information is blank on both forms, and both
N-400s in this folder are UNSIGNED drafts.
