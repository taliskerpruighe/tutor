Brent: 

It is hard to say. I am inclined to say that the spelling discrepancy would not be enough to cause a notice to bounce. Once USCIS issues the notice, it is up to the post office to make that judgment call of whether the name is misspelled or whether the person is not the same as the addressee. Usually, a discrepancy that small will not cause the individual delivering mail to think they have the wrong person. Moreover, if the post office has been delivering mail to you despite the discrepancy, it is unlikely they would have made an exception for the USCIS mail alone. Then again, we did have that other notice be returned — and we have no decent explanation for that. 

Sorry, but that is as much as I can estimate. It is certainly a matter that is up to the post office though, not USCIS: they lose control over when and how the notice gets delivered.
