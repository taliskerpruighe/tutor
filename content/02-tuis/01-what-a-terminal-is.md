# What a terminal is

*v0.1.0*

The window you are reading this in is called a **terminal**. Its full name
is a *terminal emulator*, and the second word is the interesting one. It is
emulating something. Something that used to be a physical object.

## The thing it is imitating

Before computers were personal, a computer was a room. One machine, very
expensive, shared by everybody in the building.

You did not sit at it. You sat at a desk somewhere else, in front of a
squat beige unit with a keyboard and a small green screen — the sort of
thing that looks, to modern eyes, like a microwave with ambitions. You
typed, your words travelled down a cable to the machine in the room, and
its answers came back and appeared on your screen.

That unit was the **terminal**. It had almost no intelligence of its own.
Its whole job was to send what you typed and show what came back.

Then computers got small enough to sit on a desk, and the terminal had no
reason to exist. Except that the way of working — type a line, get an
answer, type another — turned out to be a very good way to talk to a
computer. So it stayed, as software. Your Mac draws a picture of that
beige unit on the screen and behaves the way it used to behave.

> This is why you will hear the words *terminal*, *console*, *command line*,
> and *shell* used almost interchangeably. They meant distinct things once.
> In everyday speech they now mostly mean "the window where you type
> commands".

## What it actually does for you

Think of a browser. A browser is not a website. It is an app whose job is
to run websites, and which website you happen to be on is your business.

A terminal is the same shape of thing. It is an app whose job is to run
**other programs**, and which program you run is your business. In this one
window you can run:

- a **shell**, which is what greets you when it opens
- **Claude Code**
- a text editor
- this reader
- a script you wrote yourself

One at a time, or several across tabs. The terminal draws whatever they
print and passes them whatever you type. It has no opinion about the rest.

## They are not all the same

There is more than one terminal app, and they differ more than you would
expect. Some are slow to draw text. Some cannot show images. Some have
tabs; some do not. Some let you change the colours and the font; some
barely.

Your Mac comes with one called Terminal. It works. You are running a
different one, for reasons this part gets to shortly.

Press `n`.
