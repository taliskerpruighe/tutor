# The plans

*v0.2.0*

Claude is reached through an account, and an account sits on one of
four tiers: **Free**, **Pro**, **Max 5x** and **Max 20x**. The names
tell you the shape of the ladder before anything else does — each
rung is a multiple of the one below it, not a step up from it.

That is worth pausing on, because it is not how most subscriptions
work. A step up from Pro usually buys a bit more of the same thing
for a bit more money. Max does not work that way. Each tier buys
several times the usage per pound that the one below it does, so the
saving compounds as you climb rather than adding up in a straight
line. Free is a taste. Pro is a working amount for light use. Max 5x
and Max 20x are where the multiples start doing the arithmetic for
you.

## Which one this course needs

Most of what this course teaches — running Claude Code through real
matters, letting an agent chew through a bundle, keeping more than one
session open — needs Max 5x or above. Pro runs out of room quickly
once a session is doing real work rather than answering a question or
two, and Free is not really meant for this at all.

Start on Max 5x. Move to Max 20x once either of two things is true:
you are reaching for the heaviest models — Fable, most often — as a
matter of course rather than for the occasional hard question, or you
are running the kind of automated, unattended work a later part of
this course calls a **workflow**, which spends usage on its own
schedule rather than only when you are watching.

## The limit you will meet later

Every tier, Max 20x included, has a ceiling. You will meet the
practical edge of that ceiling far more often than the theoretical
one: an **account limit**, quietly capping what you can ask for
before the month or the hour resets. It is one of the two things
capable of overruling a setting you made deliberately — the effort
level you choose for an agent is a request, not a guarantee, and an
account limit is the other party allowed to say no.

None of that is a reason to avoid the higher tiers. It is a reason to
know, before you hit it, that the ceiling exists and is not a fault.
