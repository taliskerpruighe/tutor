# The companion agent

*v0.2.0*

There is a second way into this course besides pressing `n`. Open a new
Ghostty tab with `Cmd-T` and run:

```
cd ~/tutor && claude
```

That starts an agent that has already read every page you are reading
this wiki through — and, at this point, nothing else. It has not read
the wider internet on the subject and it has not read Anthropic's own
documentation. Ask it something and it answers from these pages, the
same ones you can reach with `n`, `p` and the number keys.

## What it is for

Reading in order teaches you the shape of the whole subject. Asking a
question answers the one thing you actually needed, right now, without
walking through everything that leads up to it. Both are useful, and
neither replaces the other.

*"What is the difference between an agent and a subagent?"* gets you an
answer in one paragraph, drawn from the two articles that between them
actually settle it. Reading to that same answer would mean two whole
parts of the course.

## It tells you where it got the answer

The agent names the article it drew on — part first, then section —
so you can go and read the whole thing if the short version was not
enough. That is worth doing more often than it sounds: a question
answered in isolation is a fact; the same fact inside its article has
the argument that leads up to it, which is usually the part worth
having.

## When the course has not covered something yet

Say so is the whole instruction it was given, and it does: if a
question falls outside these pages, it tells you plainly rather than
quietly answering from general knowledge and letting you assume the
answer came from here. The two are not blurred together.

## The working pattern

Keep both tabs open side by side. Read in one. Ask in the other,
whenever something in the reading is not landing, or whenever you
already know roughly what you want and reading through to it would
cost more than asking does.

Neither tab knows about the other. Asking a question in the second one
does not move your place in the first, and pressing `n` in the first
does not tell the second what you just read. You are the only thing
connecting them.

The next article is what you actually do with either tab — not reading,
not asking, but working through something with your hands on the
keyboard.

Press `n`.
