# Exercises

*v0.2.0*

Some articles are read. A few are done. An exercise is the second kind
— an article that stops explaining and asks you to open a terminal and
build something, with the rest of the article as the instructions.

You cannot finish one by reading it. That is the point of it, not a
flaw: a skill you have only read about is a fact you can repeat back,
and a skill you have used once is one you can reach for again without
this course open beside you.

## Where they sit

An exercise sits at the point in the course where you have just been
taught the piece it needs, and nowhere else — it is not a separate
section you visit later. The five articles under **Build a Chain**, in
the **Subagents** part, are the exercises that exist so far: two
custom agents and two skills, built one at a time by talking to Claude
the way the two articles before them taught, then joined into a chain
that turns a folder of documents into a chronology.

That is the only worked sequence in the course at present. More parts
will earn one as they are written; not every article needs to be
something you do rather than something you read, and most do not.

## What you keep afterwards

The chain you build in **Build a Chain** does not vanish when you
close the tab. It lands in a project folder of its own, on your
machine, and it still works the next time you point it at a different
bundle of documents. An exercise in this course is not a demonstration
you watch and discard — it is the fastest way to end up owning
something real, because building the small version teaches you what
the instructions actually meant in a way reading them never quite
does.

## What it costs you

An exercise takes longer than the article beside it — the five in
**Build a Chain** are the better part of an hour together, not the ten
minutes a normal article costs. Do not start one with five minutes
left before you have to be somewhere else. Half a chain built is
nothing you can use.
