# CLAUDE.md tips

*v0.2.1*

A `CLAUDE.md` that works is short, front-loaded, and confined to
what is true every single time — three habits, and each one is easy
to break without noticing you have broken it.

This one is short, and it reads like the very thing it recommends.

## Keep it short

*Context* already put a number on what a launch costs: the model,
the rules, the skills it can reach, and `CLAUDE.md` itself, all
sitting in the window before you have typed a word. *Context rot* is
what happens once that window fills — the agent gets worse at
everything, quietly, and never tells you it has started.

Every line left in `CLAUDE.md` is a line every session on the
walk-up path carries from the first word onward, whether that
session needed it or not. A paragraph that helped one matter six
months ago is still being paid for today by a session that has never
heard of it. If a sentence has not earned its place in the last ten
sessions, it is not standing instruction. It is clutter with a good
excuse.

## Where you put it matters

Length is not the only cost. Position is a second one, and it is the
part most people never think to manage at all.

Claude reads every word of the file, but it does not weigh every
word the same. What sits at the top gets read with full attention.
What sits at the bottom gets read on the way out, and mostly
remembered. What sits buried in the middle gets the worst of both —
read once, weighed least.

Write to that shape rather than against it.

```
  top      a short summary — what a
           skim should catch
  middle   the detail, expanding the
           summary, in the same order
  bottom   a checklist — caught on the
           way out
```

Nothing in the middle earns its place for its own sake. It exists to
make good on the summary above it, in the same order the summary
raised it, so a reader working straight through is never surprised
by what turns up next.

## What does not belong here at all

Not everything worth obeying belongs in this file, however well you
arrange it. An instruction that only applies sometimes — only in
this kind of folder, only for this kind of document — does not earn
a line in a file every session reads regardless of relevance.
Forcing it in anyway is exactly the clutter the first section warned
about, except now it is clutter that fires in sessions it was never
meant for.

There is a file built for precisely that shape of instruction.

Press `n`.
