# The permission modes there are

*v0.2.9*

*Changing permission modes* showed you the key. Here is what it steps
you through — five modes, in the same order every time, each one
answering the same question differently: how much does the session do
before it stops and checks with you?

None of the five is a setting hidden three menus deep. They sit on one
dial, and the whole of what separates them is how far along it you
are standing.

## The five, in order

| Mode | What it does |
|---|---|
| default | asks before most edits |
| accept edits | skips asking, except for changes to Claude's own files |
| auto | skips asking, judged against criteria you set |
| bypass permissions | never asks |
| plan | read-only, for working out a plan first |

## Default and accept edits

Default is where a session starts unless you have told it otherwise —
it stops before most edits and most commands, and you say yes or no
each time. That is the safest mode and the slowest one: read a file,
fine, no question asked; write to one, and it waits for you.

Accept edits removes that stop for ordinary changes and keeps it for
one category on purpose: anything touching Claude's own files,
settings included, still gets asked about. Rewriting a clause in your
draft goes straight through. Rewriting its own instructions does not.

## Auto and bypass permissions

Auto goes a step further. It skips asking, not for everything, but
against criteria — which is another way of saying it decides, and the
decision is only as good as what you have told it to be careful of.
*The boss recommends: auto mode* is about exactly that telling.

Bypass permissions removes the question outright. Nothing stops,
nothing asks, and nothing is held back for you to catch on the way
past — not a deletion, not a command that touches somewhere it should
not. It exists. Nothing else in this course recommends reaching for
it as a baseline.

## Plan

Plan mode differs in kind rather than degree — it does not skip asking
more readily than the others do, it does not act at all. *Out of the
box* already showed what a session does inside it: reads, thinks,
writes you a plan, and waits. Here it takes its place as the fifth
point on the dial, the one that touches nothing until you say go.

It earns that place rather than a category of its own because the
question is still the same one — how much does the session do before
it checks with you — and plan mode's answer to it is simply the
smallest one available. Nothing, until you approve what it proposes.
