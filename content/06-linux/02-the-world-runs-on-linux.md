# The world runs on Linux

*v0.2.0*

Linux runs on under three percent of desktops. On servers it runs the
overwhelming majority of them. Both numbers are true at once, and
neither is the whole picture — the desktop share undercounts it badly.

## Where it actually sits

The phone in your pocket runs it. Android is built on the Linux kernel,
which means more people carry Linux with them daily than have ever
seen a Linux desktop. Every website you loaded today was served by it.
So was the machine that routed your email, whoever hosts it.

**claude.ai** and **Claude Cowork** — the browser and app versions of
this same assistant — run the same way. You type into a page; the page
talks to a server; the server is a Linux machine you never see and
never asked to see. That is true of nearly everything you use that is
not itself running on your Mac.

## The one you have actually been talking to

Claude Code is the exception that proves the pattern by breaking it
halfway. The harness — the part that reads your files, runs your
commands, writes to your terminal — runs on your Mac. But the model
itself, the thing composing every answer, runs on a machine in a data
centre, and that machine is running Linux. You have been talking to it
for weeks without meeting it.

## Operating systems have their own packages article

*Packages* showed you that most of what an app does is not the
app's own code — it is packages underneath, free and open, that the
app is wrapped around. Word processing is not special; the interesting
work was written once, by someone else, and wrapped.

Operating systems turn out to work the same way, one level up. Windows
and macOS are wrapped products: closed, sold, dressed up by a company
that decided what you see. Linux is the part underneath, published
openly, with no company deciding anything, and everyone — Google
building Android, Amazon building its servers, whoever hosts your
email — free to wrap their own dressing around it.

Linux is to operating systems what packages are to apps: the layer
that actually does the work, sitting under a name you were sold. You
have been running into it your whole working life without meeting it.
Now you have.

Being common is not the same as being good, though.
